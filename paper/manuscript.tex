\begin{abstract}
Whole-organism simulators reproduce behaviour from thousands of biophysical parameters;
which of them behaviour determines is unknown. Perturbing each of a nematode model's $3076$ connectome weights, read through more
observables than parameters, behaviour determines eight directions at $90\%$ of the curvature
and $47$ at $99\%$. A \emph{Drosophila} optic-lobe model agrees at $14$ of $330$: two phyla, a few per cent
of a connectome-scale space. Chemical synapses carry $87\%$ of the curvature and gap junctions
$13\%$, and the conductances it leaves free are the fast voltage-gated calcium currents.
Cell models with known truth verify the geometry: small neuronal models are recovered
exactly, the MAPK cascade matches behaviour with its parameters wrong. Further aspects of one action add little, their subspaces overlapping more than they complement, whereas an action with a different functional demand renders different axes
stiff and reaches directions the first cannot. We
locate what behaviour leaves free.
\end{abstract}

% ============ INTRODUCTION ============
\section*{Introduction}
A central premise of mechanistic whole-organism simulators is that fitting them to behaviour recovers the biophysics that produces it. Decades of work on conductance-based models, however, from the stomatogastric ganglion \citep{prinz2004similar,marder2006variability} to the broader theory of sloppy models \citep{gutenkunst2007universally,transtrum2015perspective}, establish that many parameter sets produce indistinguishable dynamics: the map from parameters to behaviour is many-to-one \citep{goldman2001global,marder2011variability,edelman2001degeneracy}. Whether, and how strongly, this degeneracy holds at the scale of a \emph{whole-organism} nervous system, with thousands of synaptic, junctional and channel parameters driving a behaving body, has not been measured. The consequence is direct: if behaviour constrains only a low-dimensional subspace, calibration recovers that subspace and not the remainder, and what it leaves unconstrained are candidate directions along which biology can vary without behavioural consequence.

Here we measure the behavioural-equivalence manifold of four published whole-organism motor simulators (BAAIWorm \citep{wang2025baaiworm}, modWorm \citep{shlizerman2025modworm}, flybody \citep{vaxenburg2025flybody}, and NeuroMechFly~v2 / FlyGym \citep{wangchen2024neuromechfly,lobatorios2022neuromechfly}), which are built on physics engines for musculoskeletal control \citep{todorov2012mujoco}. To test generality, we apply the same measurement to a connectome-constrained \emph{Drosophila} visual circuit, the canonical crustacean pyloric population, and a mammalian controller; and to verify it against ground truth, to eleven cell models whose parameters are known exactly, spanning neuronal excitability and intracellular signalling. In each we measure the manifold of behaviourally equivalent parameter sets, identify the constrained subspace, and ask whether its geometry recurs across systems and in biology.

The components required to do this at scale are now available. Connectome-constrained whole-organism simulators rest on increasingly complete wiring diagrams \citep{white1986structure,cook2019connectome,dorkenwald2024flywire} on community simulation frameworks \citep{szigeti2014openworm,gleeson2018c302,hines1997neuron}, and on the whole-brain neural recordings they aim to reproduce \citep{kato2015global,nguyen2016whole,randi2023multimodal}; high-throughput tracking reduces behaviour to low-dimensional kinematic descriptors \citep{stephens2008dimensionality,javer2018open,pereira2020quantifying}; and parameter inference is increasingly amortised by simulation-based methods \citep{cranmer2020frontier,goncalves2020training,tejero2020sbi} or performed by black-box optimisers \citep{spall1992multivariate,hansen2001completely}; behaviour-based parameter generation for whole-organism simulators has recently been demonstrated from tracking video alone \citep{he2026pgob}, and causal-model estimation for the fly brain argues that its dominant dynamics are carried by a small fraction of the wiring \citep{pospisil2024effectome}. What none of them settles is \emph{how much} of a whole-organism parameter set behaviour determines: the identifiability question \citep{villaverde2014reverse,saltelli2008global} that sloppy-model theory answers for small systems \citep{gutenkunst2007universally,transtrum2015perspective,sethna2010sloppy} and pyloric degeneracy for a single circuit \citep{prinz2004similar,marder2006variability}, and a prerequisite for behaviourally grounded digital organisms \citep{karr2012whole,zador2023catalyzing}.

% ============ RESULTS ============
\section*{Results}

The measurement needs a system whose forward map from biophysics to behaviour is fully specified and cheap enough to probe densely. Published whole-organism simulators qualify: every synaptic, junctional and channel parameter is explicit, and behaviour can be re-simulated under controlled perturbation. The primary system is the nematode, for which two independently constructed models exist on the same connectome, so any geometry we measure can be checked against a second implementation of the same animal. Around this core we assemble a comparison set spanning four orders of parameter count, probed by one estimator (Fig.~\ref{fig:concept}).

\subsection*{Behaviour converges while parameters remain dispersed}
How much of a connectome-scale model can behaviour reach? On the BAAIWorm nematode model ($136$ cells; the $3076$ connectome weights) we perturbed every weight in turn and read the motor output as $9600$ observables, so observables outnumber parameters and the curvature rank is not capped by the measurement. The behavioural Gauss--Newton curvature has effective dimension $8$ at $90\%$ and $47$ at $99\%$ of $3076$, participation ratio $2.55$: of three thousand connection directions, behaviour constrains eight. Only $4$ weights produce no measurable change, so the flat directions are degeneracy and not unconnected parameters (Fig.~\ref{fig:manifold}a). The measurement is differentiable and noise-free: the response is proportional to the step to four significant figures over a twentyfold range, and repeat simulations are bit-identical (Supplementary~\S1.5). Nor is it tied to one parameter point, or to one layer of the model: at four displaced operating points the effective dimension stays at $8$--$12$ and the stiff subspaces coincide (cosine $0.89$, random $0.15$), and reading the same $3076$ connections through the model's own muscle output instead of the motor-neuron potentials gives $3$ / $11$ at participation ratio $2.53$, so the readout nearest behaviour is the more concentrated of the two (Supplementary~\S5.3).

\subsection*{The behavioural-equivalence manifold is low-dimensional}
The same geometry appears at every parameter scale, provided the observable count does not limit it. A Gauss--Newton Hessian from $n_{\mathrm{obs}}$ observables has rank at most $n_{\mathrm{obs}}$, so a low effective dimension read through a few summary statistics says as much about the measurement as about the model. On the $48$ FlyGym actuator gains, ten summary scalars give $1$, whereas $4200$ joint angles give $16$ ($90\%$) / $38$ ($99\%$) against a noise floor of $7$ (Supplementary~\S5.1); on modWorm the answer does not move between six scalars and $7{,}200$ posture observables. Across parameter counts of $4$, $12$, $48$ and $3076$, behaviour is set by a low-dimensional stiff subspace embedded in a high-dimensional flat manifold (Fig.~\ref{fig:perchannel}b). The low dimension is not an artefact of where the spectral mass is cut. Sweeping it from $80\%$ to $99\%$ moves the modWorm mechanism count only from $2$ to $4$ of $7$ and the BAAIWorm per-channel count from $1$ to $3$ of $20$, with the threshold-free participation ratios, $2.23$ and $1.34$, inside those ranges (Supplementary~\S6.9). Nor is it an artefact of the estimator: scrambling each behaviour's Jacobian while preserving its marginal distribution raises the effective dimension to the ceiling and flattens the spectrum, and the estimator returns the correct answer on constructed spectra of known rank (Supplementary~\S6.8).

\subsection*{The manifold is the neural control of eigenworm posture}
The nematode behavioural manifold is the parameter-space image of the animal's approximately four-dimensional postural repertoire. Real \emph{C.~elegans} posture from the OpenWorm Movement Database occupies $3.79$--$4.14$ effective dimensions, four eigenworms capturing $94$--$96\%$ of postural variance, reproducing the classical eigenworm decomposition \citep{stephens2008dimensionality}. Two independently built worm models generate posture overlapping this basis, at mean principal-angle $\cos\,0.77$ for modWorm and $\cos\,0.68$--$0.69$ for BAAIWorm. The models' repertoires are lower-dimensional than the animal's ($1.66$ and $2.76$--$3.30$ against $3.79$--$4.14$), and the agreement holds for the subspace rather than for individual modes (alignments $0.30$, $0.02$, $0.57$, $0.24$), exceeding random four-dimensional subspaces (modWorm $p{=}0.001$, BAAIWorm $p{=}0.04$ within a conservative ten-dimensional ambient; $p{<}10^{-4}$ in the full space; Supplementary~\S6.1). Within each model the stiff mechanisms, not the sloppy ones, displaced the eigenworm trajectory. The effective rank tracks the repertoire's intrinsic dimensionality rather than the number of observables recorded: $7{,}200$--$14{,}400$ posture observables in place of six summary kinematics leave the effective dimension at $2$--$3$ of $7$ (Supplementary~\S6.2). The correspondence is empirical rather than a bound, so the identifiable subspace is related to, and not identical with, the neural control of the postural repertoire (Fig.~\ref{fig:tiling}a).

\subsection*{More of one action converges; a different action reaches elsewhere}
How much can a repertoire add? The question separates into two with opposite answers. Further aspects of one action converge on the subspace that action already determines. Four distinct locomotor presets each constrained $2$--$4$ of $7$ mechanism directions at mean pairwise stiff-subspace $\cos\,0.54$, against $\cos\,0.31$ and a span of $3.50$ dimensions ($95\%$ interval $3.00$--$3.88$) for randomly oriented directions: more aligned than chance ($p{=}0.016$ for the cosine, $p{=}0.020$ for the span, $20{,}000$ draws) while still spanning $2.98$ of $4$ (Supplementary~\S6.4). Their union reached $5$ of $7$ at $99\%$ of the curvature and $2$ at $90\%$; across twelve behaviours it saturated at $4$ and $2$, leaving $2$--$3$ directions that no behaviour constrains, an irreducible degenerate core. The fly saturates the same way, $16.5$ to $19$ of $48$ actuator directions from one behaviour to four. Measuring one action more finely therefore approaches a limit, and that limit is the action's own subspace.

A different action does not. In BAAIWorm, perturbing the chemosensory parameters altered behaviour $7.15\times$ more during chemotaxis (sensitivity $0.872$) than during plain locomotion in a weak gradient ($0.122$), whereas perturbing the motor parameters showed the opposite contrast ($0.109$ versus $0.357$; Supplementary~\S6.3): the axis a behaviour renders stiff is the axis its function requires. As subspaces, chemotaxis renders four directions of the circuit distinguishable and locomotion four, while the two combined render six, so each reaches two directions the other cannot---a rank statement, requiring no threshold (Supplementary~\S6.10). The constrained parameters change identity accordingly: of the four neuron classes carrying most curvature the two actions share only the ventral-cord A-type motor neurons, exchanging chemosensory AWC and interneuron AIZ for the inhibitory DD and VD. This matches the anatomical separation of the chemosensory navigation circuit from the ventral-cord locomotor circuit \citep{gray2005circuit,bargmann2012beyond}, and models in which distinct premotor and motor elements generate the rhythm \citep{wen2012proprioceptive,fouad2018distributed,izquierdo2018connecting}. Stiff and sloppy directions are distributed parameter combinations rather than single parameters (stiff participation $3.42$, sloppy $4.68$ of $7$; Supplementary~\S6.7), so the residual core is a biological neutral space and not an unused parameter (Fig.~\ref{fig:tiling}b,c).

\subsection*{The same geometry in a second phylum}
The organisation is not specific to the nematode, and not to motor control. The flyvis model of the \emph{Drosophila} optic lobe is built on the fly connectome and carries $734$ free parameters in three groups: neuron biases, membrane time constants and synaptic strengths. Probed across all three and read through its visual output, its curvature has effective dimension $14$ at $90\%$ and $32$ at $99\%$ of $330$ sampled parameters, with a response proportional to the perturbation across two decades of step size (Supplementary~\S5.7). A visual circuit in one phylum and a motor circuit in another therefore return the same answer, a few per cent of a connectome-scale parameter space. Within the fly, the FlyGym walking controller gives $16$ of $48$ actuator gains read through $4200$ joint-angle observables, rising to $19$ as behaviours are added (Supplementary~\S6.5), so the two fly models agree with each other as well as with the worm. A mammalian controller shows the same rising-and-saturating union over six behaviours; its observables are fewer than its parameters, so we read structure from it and not dimensions, and a fly-larva probe failed its noise check and is withdrawn (Supplementary~\S6.5). Absolute dimensions are not comparable across models parameterised differently; the structure and not the count is what recurs.

\subsection*{Ground truth verifies the geometry where it can be known}
The whole-organism models have no true parameter vector; at the cell scale the curvature can be checked against truth. Across eleven cell models measured identically and recovered from random starts independent of the truth, both ends of the geometry appear: the $2$--$4$-parameter neuronal models come back exactly (error $<10^{-4}$), while the canonical MAPK cascade \citep{kholodenko2000negative}, the family in which sloppiness was first described \citep{gutenkunst2007universally}, matches behaviour to $10^{-3}$ with its parameters $40\%$ from the truth, farther than they started. A perfect behavioural fit does not imply recovered parameters. Truth also bounds the effective dimension's meaning: across a parameter-count $\times$ observable grid on one neuron it is $1$ in every cell while achieved recovery error spans $4$--$56\%$; it measures curvature concentration, not an upper bound on recoverable parameters (Supplementary~\S7). On the worm model an independent multi-start probe ranks the identifiable axes identically ($\rho{=}0.96$, $p{=}5\times10^{-4}$), with simulation-based calibration as a control.

Ground truth also resolves an apparent disagreement with the degeneracy literature, which reports conductance-based models to be far less identifiable than the exact recovery above. The disagreement is one of protocol. Measuring a single Hodgkin--Huxley neuron through twelve combinations of parameter count and assay shows that what moves the answer is how many conductances are searched: three recovered against a seven-point current--frequency curve end $3.8\%$ from the truth, eight against the identical assay $38\%$ away, inside the degenerate range the literature describes. Reporting the parameter count alongside the assay makes the two comparable. Nor is the small constrained fraction of the connectome-scale model a consequence of its size: adding identical cells to a fixed sparse network, from $6$ to $192$ parameters, moves the constrained fraction only from $0.33$ to $0.30$ while the effective dimension itself rises at close to one direction per added cell, so the fraction is set by what behaviour transmits, not by how many parameters exist (Supplementary~\S7.3, \S7.4).

\subsection*{Stiff and sloppy directions are biophysically interpretable}
Decomposing the BAAIWorm curvature over the model's named conductances, read through the full motor-neuron trajectory rather than a handful of scalars, gives an effective dimension of $3$ ($90\%$) / $6$ ($99\%$) over its sixteen named conductances, with elasticities spanning three orders of magnitude. The partition by mechanism is clear: the stiffest axes are the inward rectifier IRK, the sodium leak NCA and the potassium conductances EGL-36 and EGL-2, whereas the fast voltage-gated calcium channels EGL-19 ($1.77$) and UNC-2 ($0.17$) are among the sloppiest, together with SLO-2 ($0.57$--$0.78$) and SLO-1/EGL-19 ($0.09$); the other calcium-activated potassium conductances, KCNL ($15.1$) and SLO-1/UNC-2 ($6.68$), sit mid-rank (Supplementary~\S5.2). Behaviour is thus determined by how cells are connected and by their passive and slow properties, and is largely insensitive to the fast voltage-gated calcium conductances, the currents whose compensation and variability are documented in small circuits \citep{marder2006variability}. Most of the degeneracy lies in the connectome: $3068$ of $3076$ directions are flat, and the surviving curvature is uneven across connection types, $1992$ chemical synapses carrying $87\%$ of it and $1084$ gap junctions $13\%$, so per connection a synapse matters about five times more, with per-connection elasticities spanning seven orders (Supplementary~\S5.3). Probed identically, modWorm (coupled-ODE, $7$ mechanisms) and BAAIWorm (NEURON, $20$ named channels) agree that the network-connectivity axes are stiff (stiff fraction $0.83$ / $0.87$) and the single-cell biophysical axes sloppy ($0.08$ / $0.16$), so ``connectivity stiff, single-cell sloppy'' reproduces across independent implementations of the same organism (Supplementary~\S6.6).

\subsection*{Convergent observations in independent biological data}
Four datasets that use none of our forward models show the same structure at four levels of description (Fig.~\ref{fig:external}). 
\emph{(1) Pyloric degeneracy.} The same probe applied to the Prinz--Marder population of conductance-based models producing one triphasic rhythm \citep{prinz2004similar} gives a $31$-parameter spectrum over ten orders of magnitude, effective dimension $2.13$, stiff on the leak and inhibitory-synapse conductances and sloppy on the fast sodium conductances, the currents known to be compensated in this circuit.
\emph{(2) Neuronal property variability.} Across $n{=}1914$ Allen cortical neurons, electrophysiological properties span the same gradient (coefficient of variation $0.08$--$1.85$, resting potential stiff, firing rate sloppy) on a manifold of participation dimension $4.78$ of $11$.
\emph{(3) Gene conservation.} Across $40$ \emph{C.~elegans} ion-channel and synaptic genes, ortholog identity spans $77.9$--$99.9\%$, the most conserved being the gap-junction gene \texttt{unc-9}, consistent with the connectivity axes being stiff in both worm models.
\emph{(4) Behavioural variation.} Across $n{=}61$ isogenic N2 worms ($683$ Tierpsy features), cross-individual behaviour occupies a participation dimension of $9.75$: genetically identical animals vary on a roughly ten-dimensional manifold. What biology conserves and what it permits to vary are ordered consistently with the models' identifiable and degenerate axes.

% ============ DISCUSSION ============
\section*{Discussion}
We report a measurement of what behaviour can and cannot identify in whole-organism nervous-system models. In the nematode model built on the connectome, behaviour constrains eight of three thousand connection directions, and the conductances it barely sees are the fast voltage-gated calcium currents. Measuring further aspects of one action adds little: in the fly the behaviours compared are descending turn commands driving one pattern generator, in the nematode touch at two sites and two intensities, and the subspaces they determine overlap far more than they complement. That overlap is itself the finding, measuring degeneracy without choosing a threshold. Actions that engage different circuits behave otherwise: chemotaxis and locomotion, driven independently by moving the food source, render different axes stiff, four directions each and six together. Escape and feeding remain untested, for want of a simulator that drives them independently, which is a requirement on the models before it is a question about the animals.

The organising principle is that behaviour reports on a small, structured part of a model. Any one behaviour pins a few stiff parameter combinations, and different behaviours pin the combinations their function requires; between aspects of one action the subspaces overlap heavily and the union saturates at once.

This recasts degeneracy as a measurable resource rather than a fixed limit, and says where parameter recovery should be posed. What behaviour identifies is not a parameter vector but a few coordinates on it; in the cell models where the truth is known, recovery along those coordinates is exact while recovery of the full vector is not. The identified set grows with the number of functionally distinct actions a simulator can be driven through, making assay diversity rather than recording duration the quantity to optimise, but it does not grow without limit: after the whole repertoire has been used a residual degenerate core remains, and those are the candidate neutral directions.

The result lifts the sloppy-models picture \citep{gutenkunst2007universally,transtrum2015perspective,sethna2010sloppy} and pyloric degeneracy \citep{prinz2004similar,marder2011variability} from single circuits to behaving whole organisms, and explains why behaviour-based calibration recovers a working parameter set without recovering unique biophysical values: it recovers the stiff union, not the core. The independent datasets indicate that the unconstrained directions tend also to be the more variable and less conserved ones in biology, consistent with the biophysical diversity documented within identified cell types \citep{gjorgjieva2016computational}.

That two connectome-constrained models in different phyla, one visual and one motor, return the same few per cent indicates the number reflects how much of a nervous system behaviour transmits rather than a property of one simulator.

How much was recorded sets what can be found, and by how much is now measurable. A Gauss--Newton curvature built from $n_{\mathrm{obs}}$ observables has rank at most $n_{\mathrm{obs}}$, and the models track that bound: reading the visual model through fewer output channels moves its effective dimension almost one for one with the channel count, and the FlyGym figure moves from $1$ to $16$ when six summary scalars are replaced by $4200$ joint angles. An effective dimension reported through fewer observables than parameters is therefore a property of the assay, so we report one only where observables outnumber parameters, and give the observable count, the step-size scan and the noise floor with every figure.

Three limitations bound these results. The choice of observables is not unique, though re-measuring with $7{,}200$--$14{,}400$ posture observables in place of six summary kinematics returns the same $2$--$3$ of $7$ (Supplementary~\S6.2). The per-channel decomposition scales each conductance type uniformly across cells rather than cell by cell, though two independently built worm models agree on the class-level partition. The alignment with the biological datasets holds at the level of dimensionality and direction rather than per eigenvector, and behaviour does not uniquely localise which mechanism was perturbed, the detectable mechanisms being mutually confusable (Supplementary~\S5.4, \S5.5).

The strongest outstanding test is prospective: predicting from the model which perturbations should produce the largest behavioural change, and testing that ordering on data not used to build it. Such a test requires perturbations resolved at the coupled parameter combinations the geometry identifies rather than at single genes or channels, and defining those endpoints in advance protects the statistics when independent samples are few.

Behavioural identifiability is therefore a quantity that can be measured rather than assumed, and its extent is set by the diversity of behaviours elicited. The stiff subspace is the part of a fitted model that the assay pins down and that can be read as biology; the free directions name what it leaves open and which further behaviours would close. The same geometry prices an assay before it is run: one that adds another aspect of the same action re-measures known combinations, one that changes what the circuit must do reaches new ones. This applies to any mechanistic simulator fitted to behaviour.

\section*{Methods}
\textbf{Manifold drift.} For each simulator, $N$ optimisations (separable CMA-ES or SPSA) from independent starts are fitted to one fixed behavioural target; we report behavioural recovery, the normalised per-dimension dispersion of the $N$ recovered parameter vectors, and the PCA participation dimension of the solution cloud. \textbf{Behavioural curvature.} All curvature reported here is the Gauss--Newton form $H=J^\top W J$, built from the finite-difference observable Jacobian $J_{ik}=\partial\,\mathrm{obs}_i/\partial\theta_k$ and a diagonal positive semi-definite whitening matrix $W$ of inverse per-observable scales. $H$ is therefore positive semi-definite by construction and equals the Fisher information of the parameters given the behavioural observables, so its eigenvalues are non-negative and its effective dimension is the local practical-identifiability rank in the standard sense \citep{villaverde2014reverse,gutenkunst2007universally}. Eigenvalues are obtained by direct eigendecomposition (low dimension) or Lanczos iteration (high dimension). The effective dimension at level $q$ is the smallest $k$ such that $\sum_{i\le k}\lambda_i \ge q\sum_i \lambda_i$ with eigenvalues sorted in decreasing order; we report $q=0.90$ and $q=0.99$, together with the threshold-free participation ratio $(\sum_i\lambda_i)^2/\sum_i\lambda_i^2$ (Supplementary~\S6.9). Curvature is evaluated at the published operating point, and its point dependence is measured directly: on the connectome-scale model, four displaced operating points give effective dimension $8$--$12$ with stiff subspaces coinciding across points at cosine $0.89$ against a random null of $0.15$ (Supplementary~\S5.3); on modWorm, five displaced optima and twenty-four random points all give a low effective dimension (Supplementary~\S6.8). A direction counts as flat when $\lambda$ falls to the finite-difference floor; residual eigenvalues of magnitude below that floor, of either sign, are numerical noise and are treated as zero. We adopt it as the primary cross-simulator metric because it is robust to the choice of observable and parameterisation, unlike a raw perturbation-sensitivity ratio, and because the manifold-drift probe provides an independent, optimisation-based cross-check that agrees with it ($\rho{=}0.96$). \textbf{Eigenworm identity.} Real \emph{C.~elegans} skeletons (OWMD N2) and model body postures are reduced to tangent-angle eigenmodes by PCA; we report the posture effective dimension, the top-four cumulative variance, and the principal-angle cosine between model and real eigenworm subspaces, with significance assessed against random subspaces of matched dimension. \textbf{Complementary tiling and saturation.} Per-behaviour stiff subspaces are taken as the leading Hessian eigenvectors under each behavioural target; we report pairwise stiff-subspace cosines, the union effective dimension over a behaviour set, and the saturation curve of union dimension against the number of behaviours. Context dependence (BAAIWorm) is the ratio of a parameter class's behavioural sensitivity between two behaviours (chemotaxis versus weak-gradient locomotion). The same union/saturation construction is applied to the rodent (Virtual Rodent / dm\_control). \textbf{Cell-scale ground truth.} Eleven published cell models (five neuronal, six biochemical oscillators) are measured with the identical estimator and recovered from random starts $\theta^{*}e^{\,\mathcal{U}(-\ln f,\ln f)}$ at $f{=}1.5$--$10$, with a phase-invariant feature loss computed at full resolution; a fit counts as converged below behavioural distance $0.02$ and only converged fits contribute parameter errors (Supplementary~\S7). \textbf{Calibration.} Per-dimension rank-based simulation-based calibration \citep{talts2018validating,hermans2022sbc} of an amortised neural posterior; a dimension passes when the per-axis Kolmogorov--Smirnov test is non-significant at $\alpha{=}0.05$. \textbf{External datasets.} The Prinz--Marder pyloric model population re-gated to the triphasic rhythm and probed with the identical Hessian-eigenspectrum method; Allen Cell Types electrophysiology; WormBase ParaSite / Ensembl Metazoa ortholog identities; and OpenWorm Movement Database isogenic-N2 Tierpsy features. All external data are public, and no simulator is used in datasets (1)--(4). Full specifications, cohort sizes and reproduction commands are given in the Supplementary Information.
