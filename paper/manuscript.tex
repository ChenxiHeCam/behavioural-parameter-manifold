\title{\textbf{How much of a nervous-system model does behaviour identify?}}

\begin{abstract}
Whole-organism simulators reproduce behaviour from thousands of parameters;
which of them behaviour determines is unknown. Perturbing each of a nematode model's $3076$ connectome weights, with observables
outnumbering parameters, behaviour determines eight directions at $90\%$ of the curvature,
$47$ at $99\%$. A \emph{Drosophila} optic-lobe model agrees at $14$ of $330$: in two phyla, behaviour
leaves the great majority of a connectome-scale space free. Chemical synapses carry $87\%$ of the curvature, gap junctions $13\%$; the conductances left free are the calcium currents EGL-19 and UNC-2.
Cell models with known truth verify it: small neuronal models recover exactly, MAPK matches behaviour with wrong parameters. Further aspects of one action add little, their subspaces overlapping more than they
complement, whereas an action with different functional demands reaches directions the first cannot. What a fit to behaviour pins down is measurable, set by the diversity of behaviours
elicited and separable from what it leaves free.
\end{abstract}

% ============ INTRODUCTION ============
\section*{Introduction}
Whole-organism simulators are built against the animal's own data: virtual flies fly and walk by imitating recorded trajectories or replaying recorded leg kinematics \citep{vaxenburg2025flybody,wangchen2024neuromechfly,lobatorios2022neuromechfly}, and a virtual nematode tuned to electrophysiology is judged by whether the assembled loop crawls and chemotaxes like the worm \citep{wang2025baaiworm}. Reading such a model as the animal's biophysics assumes that reproducing behaviour determines the parameters that produce it. Decades of work on conductance-based models, however, from the stomatogastric ganglion \citep{prinz2004similar,marder2006variability} to the broader theory of sloppy models \citep{gutenkunst2007universally,transtrum2015perspective}, establish that many parameter sets produce indistinguishable dynamics: the map from parameters to behaviour is many-to-one \citep{goldman2001global,marder2011variability,edelman2001degeneracy}. Whether, and how strongly, this degeneracy holds at the scale of a \emph{whole-organism} nervous system, with thousands of synaptic, junctional and channel parameters driving a behaving body, has not been measured. The consequence is direct: if behaviour constrains only a low-dimensional subspace, calibration recovers that subspace and not the remainder, and what it leaves unconstrained are candidate directions along which biology can vary without behavioural consequence.

Here we measure the behavioural-equivalence manifold of four published whole-organism motor simulators (BAAIWorm \citep{wang2025baaiworm}, modWorm \citep{shlizerman2025modworm}, flybody \citep{vaxenburg2025flybody}, and NeuroMechFly~v2 / FlyGym \citep{wangchen2024neuromechfly,lobatorios2022neuromechfly}), which are built on physics engines for musculoskeletal control \citep{todorov2012mujoco}. To test generality, we apply the same measurement to a connectome-constrained \emph{Drosophila} visual circuit, the canonical crustacean pyloric population, and a mammalian controller; and to verify it against ground truth, to eleven cell models whose parameters are known exactly, spanning neuronal excitability and intracellular signalling. In each we measure the manifold of behaviourally equivalent parameter sets, identify the constrained subspace, and ask whether its geometry recurs across systems and in biology.

The components required at this scale are now available. Connectome-constrained whole-organism simulators combine increasingly complete wiring diagrams \citep{white1986structure,cook2019connectome,dorkenwald2024flywire} with community simulation frameworks \citep{szigeti2014openworm,gleeson2018c302,hines1997neuron} and whole-brain neural recordings \citep{kato2015global,nguyen2016whole,randi2023multimodal}. High-throughput tracking reduces behaviour to low-dimensional kinematic descriptors \citep{stephens2008dimensionality,javer2018open,pereira2020quantifying}, while parameter inference is amortised by simulation-based methods \citep{cranmer2020frontier,goncalves2020training,tejero2020sbi} or performed by black-box optimisers \citep{spall1992multivariate,hansen2001completely}. Behaviour-based parameter generation from tracking video has been demonstrated for whole-organism simulators \citep{he2026pgob}, and causal-model estimation attributes the fly brain's dominant dynamics to a small fraction of its wiring \citep{pospisil2024effectome}. What none of these components settles is \emph{how much} of a whole-organism parameter set behaviour determines: the identifiability question \citep{villaverde2014reverse,saltelli2008global} that sloppy-model theory answers for small systems \citep{gutenkunst2007universally,transtrum2015perspective,sethna2010sloppy} and pyloric degeneracy for a single circuit \citep{prinz2004similar,marder2006variability}, and a prerequisite for behaviourally grounded digital organisms \citep{karr2012whole,zador2023catalyzing}.

% ============ RESULTS ============
\section*{Results}

The measurement requires a system whose forward map from biophysics to behaviour is fully specified and inexpensive enough for dense perturbation. Published whole-organism simulators qualify: every synaptic, junctional and channel parameter is explicit, and behaviour can be re-simulated under controlled perturbation. The primary system is the nematode, for which two independently constructed models exist on the same connectome, so any geometry we measure can be checked against a second implementation of the same animal. Around this core we assemble a comparison set spanning four orders of parameter count, all measured with one estimator (Fig.~\ref{fig:concept}).

\subsection*{Behaviour converges while parameters remain dispersed}
How much of a connectome-scale model can behaviour reach? On the BAAIWorm nematode model ($136$ cells; the $3076$ connectome weights) we perturbed every weight in turn and recorded the model's motor command, the membrane potentials of $80$ motor neurons over time, as $9600$ observables, so observables outnumber parameters and the curvature rank is not capped by the measurement. The behavioural Gauss--Newton curvature has effective dimension $8$ at $90\%$ and $47$ at $99\%$ of $3076$, participation ratio $2.55$: eight directions carry $90\%$ of the curvature of three thousand. Only $4$ weights produce no measurable change, so the flat directions are degeneracy, not unconnected parameters (Fig.~\ref{fig:measurement}a). The measurement is differentiable and noise-free: the response is proportional to the step to within $0.7\%$ over a twentyfold range, and repeat simulations are bit-identical (Supplementary~\S1.5). The number describes a region and not a point, and a circuit and not a readout. At four displaced operating points (a common $300$-connection subsample) the dimension stays at $8$--$12$ and the stiff subspaces coincide at cosine $0.89$ against a random null of $0.16$; measured from the model's own motor output, the $96$ muscle activations, the full $3076$ weights give $3$ / $11$, so the layer closest to behaviour is the more concentrated (Supplementary~\S5.3).

\subsection*{The behavioural-equivalence manifold is low-dimensional}
The same geometry appears at every parameter scale, provided the observable count does not limit it. A Gauss--Newton Hessian from $n_{\mathrm{obs}}$ observables has rank at most $n_{\mathrm{obs}}$, so a low effective dimension computed from a few summary statistics says as much about the measurement as about the model. On the $48$ FlyGym actuator gains, ten summary scalars give $1$, whereas $4200$ joint angles give $11$ ($90\%$) / $31$ ($99\%$) against a noise floor of $7$ (Supplementary~\S5.1); on modWorm the answer does not move between six scalars and $7{,}200$ posture observables. Across parameter counts of $4$, $12$, $48$ and $3076$, behaviour is set by a low-dimensional stiff subspace embedded in a high-dimensional flat manifold (Fig.~\ref{fig:measurement}e). Neither threshold nor estimator sets that number. Sweeping the spectral-mass threshold from $80\%$ to $99\%$ moves the modWorm count from $2$ to $4$ of its five mechanisms with measurable effects and the BAAIWorm class-gain count from $1$ to $3$ of $20$, with the threshold-free participation ratios, $2.23$ and $1.34$, inside those ranges. Scrambling each behaviour's Jacobian while preserving its marginals raises the dimension from $2.5$--$3.0$ to $4.0$ and the participation ratio from $1.8$ to $3.5$--$3.9$, and the estimator recovers constructed spectra of known rank exactly at $99\%$, within one at $90\%$ (Supplementary~\S6.4, \S6.8, \S6.9).

\subsection*{The manifold is the neural control of eigenworm posture}
The nematode behavioural manifold is the parameter-space image of the animal's approximately four-dimensional postural repertoire. Real \emph{C.~elegans} posture from the OpenWorm Movement Database occupies $3.79$--$4.14$ effective dimensions, four eigenworms capturing $94$--$96\%$ of postural variance, reproducing the classical eigenworm decomposition \citep{stephens2008dimensionality}. Two independently built worm models generate posture overlapping this basis, at mean principal-angle $\cos\,0.77$ for modWorm and $\cos\,0.68$--$0.69$ for BAAIWorm. The models' repertoires are lower-dimensional than the animal's ($1.66$ and $2.76$--$3.30$), and the agreement holds for the subspace rather than for individual modes, exceeding random four-dimensional subspaces (modWorm $p{=}0.001$, BAAIWorm $p{=}0.04$ within a conservative ten-dimensional ambient; Supplementary~\S6.1). Stiff mechanisms displace the eigenworm trajectory more than sloppy ones, by a ratio of $1.17$. What the effective rank tracks is the repertoire's intrinsic dimensionality and not the number of observables recorded: replacing six summary kinematics with $7{,}200$--$14{,}400$ posture observables leaves it at $2$--$3$ of $7$ (Supplementary~\S6.2). The identifiable subspace and the neural control of posture are therefore two descriptions of one low-dimensional object, matched empirically across two models and one animal (Fig.~\ref{fig:tiling}a).

\subsection*{Functionally different actions extend the identified set}
Adding behaviours produces two outcomes. Further aspects of one action converge on its existing subspace. Four distinct locomotor presets each constrained $2$--$4$ of $7$ mechanism directions at mean pairwise stiff-subspace $\cos\,0.54$, against $\cos\,0.31$ and a span of $3.50$ dimensions ($95\%$ interval $3.00$--$3.88$) for randomly oriented directions: more aligned than chance (Holm-adjusted $p{=}0.047$ for the cosine, $p{=}0.041$ for the span, $20{,}000$ draws) while still spanning $2.98$ of $4$ (Supplementary~\S6.4). Together they reached $5$ of $7$ at $99\%$ of the curvature and $2$ at $90\%$; across twelve behaviours the combined curvature saturated at $4$ and $2$, leaving one of the five live mechanism directions unconstrained by any tested behaviour, beside the two with no measurable effect. The fly behaves the same way, $12.25$ to $13.0$ of $48$ actuator directions over four commands, an increment of $0.75$ against a single-behaviour spread of $\mathrm{sd}\,3.0$. Measuring one action more finely therefore approaches a limit, and that limit is the action's own subspace.

A functionally different action extends that subspace. In BAAIWorm, perturbing the chemosensory parameters altered behaviour $7.15\times$ more during chemotaxis (sensitivity $0.872$) than during plain locomotion in a weak gradient ($0.122$), whereas perturbing the motor parameters showed the opposite contrast ($0.109$ versus $0.357$; Supplementary~\S6.3): the axis a behaviour renders stiff is the axis its function requires. As subspaces, chemotaxis renders four directions distinguishable and locomotion four, the two combined six, so each reaches two the other cannot. That is a rank statement and needs no threshold; the two directions it adds are the smallest of the six, at $0.30\%$ and $0.26\%$ of the combined curvature against $1.51\%$ and $1.03\%$ for the third and fourth, so they enter the spectrum alongside the directions already counted rather than at its floor (Supplementary~\S6.10). The constrained parameters change identity accordingly: of the four neuron classes carrying most curvature the two actions share only the ventral-cord A-type motor neurons, exchanging AWC and AIZ for the inhibitory DD and VD. This matches the anatomical separation of the chemosensory navigation circuit from the ventral-cord locomotor circuit \citep{gray2005circuit,bargmann2012beyond}, and models in which premotor and motor elements generate the rhythm \citep{wen2012proprioceptive,izquierdo2018connecting}. Where the eigenvectors are resolved, the stiffest and third directions are combinations of mechanism classes (participation $2.88$ and $3.25$ of $16$, the second at $1.62$) while the sloppy end approaches a single class ($1.02$), so what behaviour leaves free is individual conductances rather than a coupled combination (Supplementary~\S6.7; Fig.~\ref{fig:tiling}b,c). In this parameterisation the free directions are therefore closable one conductance at a time, which the collective account of sloppiness \citep{gutenkunst2007universally,sethna2010sloppy} does not anticipate.

\subsection*{The same geometry in a second phylum}
The same organisation appears in a second phylum and in a sensory rather than a motor circuit. The flyvis model of the \emph{Drosophila} optic lobe is built on the fly connectome and carries $734$ free parameters in three groups: neuron biases, membrane time constants and synaptic strengths. With all three groups perturbed and the response recorded across its $64$ output channels, the curvature places $14$ directions at $90\%$ and $32$ at $99\%$ over $330$ sampled parameters, both far below the $64$-channel cap, the rule every capped measurement here follows. The stiff types are the motion-detecting T4 and T5 cells and their medulla inputs and the sloppy ones the photoreceptors and lamina wide-field cells, so the pathway that performs the computation is behaviourally load-bearing and the input gain is not (Fig.~\ref{fig:flyvis}; Supplementary~\S5.7, \S5.8). A visual circuit in one phylum and a motor circuit in another therefore return the same answer. Within the fly, the FlyGym walking controller gives $11$ of $48$ actuator gains from $4200$ joint-angle observables, and adding behaviours does not move it beyond the single-behaviour spread (Supplementary~\S6.5), so the two fly models agree with each other as well as with the worm. A mammalian controller's curvature accumulated across six behaviours rises from $3.7$ to $12.0$ of $38$ actuators and has not saturated within the set tested (Supplementary~\S6.5). Models parameterised differently are not comparable by absolute dimension, and what recurs across all of them is the shape: a stiff subspace of a few directions inside a flat manifold of hundreds.

\subsection*{Ground truth verifies the geometry where it can be known}
The whole-organism models have no true parameter vector; at the cell scale the curvature can be checked against truth. Across eleven cell models measured identically and recovered from random starts independent of the truth, both ends of the geometry appear: the $2$--$4$-parameter neuronal models come back exactly (error $<10^{-4}$), while the canonical MAPK cascade \citep{kholodenko2000negative}, the family in which sloppiness was first described \citep{gutenkunst2007universally}, matches behaviour with its parameters $40\%$ from the truth, farther than they started. A perfect behavioural fit does not imply recovered parameters, and converged error rises with parameter count across the eleven models ($\rho{=}0.70$, $p{=}0.017$). Truth also bounds the effective dimension's meaning: across a parameter-count $\times$ observable grid on one neuron it is $1$ in every cell while recovery error among behaviour-matched fits spans $4$--$38\%$; it measures curvature concentration, not an upper bound on recoverable parameters (Supplementary~\S7). On the worm model an independent multi-start generation probe \citep{he2026pgob} ranks the identifiable axes identically ($\rho{=}0.96$, $p{=}5\times10^{-4}$), with simulation-based calibration as a control (Fig.~\ref{fig:cellgt}). 

Recovery depends on protocol: on one Hodgkin--Huxley neuron across twelve parameter-count and assay combinations, what moves the answer is how many conductances are searched. Three recovered against a seven-point current--frequency curve end $3.8\%$ from the truth, eight against the identical assay $38\%$ away, inside the degenerate range the literature describes. Nor is the small constrained fraction of the connectome-scale model a consequence of its size: adding identical cells to a fixed sparse network, from $6$ to $192$ parameters, moves that fraction only from $0.33$ to $0.30$ while the dimension itself rises at close to one direction per added cell (Supplementary~\S7.3, \S7.4).

\subsection*{Stiff and sloppy directions are biophysically interpretable}
Scaling each named conductance uniformly across cells and measuring the full motor-neuron trajectory gives an effective dimension of $3$ ($90\%$) / $6$ ($99\%$) over sixteen conductance-class gains, with elasticities spanning three orders of magnitude. The stiffest axes are the inward rectifier IRK, the sodium leak NCA and the potassium conductances EGL-36 and EGL-2, whereas the high-voltage-activated calcium channels EGL-19 ($1.77$) and UNC-2 ($0.17$) are among the sloppiest while CCA-1 ($39.1$) is not, with SLO-2 ($0.57$--$0.78$) and SLO-1/EGL-19 ($0.09$); KCNL ($15.1$) and SLO-1/UNC-2 ($6.68$) sit mid-rank (Supplementary~\S5.2). Behaviour is thus set by how cells are connected and by their passive and slow properties (Fig.~\ref{fig:measurement}d), and is largely insensitive to EGL-19 and UNC-2, currents documented as homeostatically compensated in small circuits \citep{marder2006variability}. These identities are resolved at the level of the mechanism class. Refining one mechanism to per-cell resolution holds the dimension at $1$--$2$ while placing $99.7\%$ of the curvature outside the uniform direction, so the number survives the refinement and the question of which cells carry it is a separate measurement. Most of the degeneracy lies in the connectome: beyond the leading $47$ directions the remaining $3029$ together carry under $1\%$ of the curvature, and the surviving curvature is uneven, $1992$ chemical synapses carrying $87\%$ against $13\%$ from $1084$ gap junctions, so per connection a synapse matters $3.6$ times more. The medians differ by $1.20\times$, which places the split in the heavy tail of strong connections rather than in the typical one (Supplementary~\S5.3). Under the same per-mechanism measurement, modWorm (coupled-ODE, $7$ mechanisms) and BAAIWorm (NEURON, $20$ named channels) agree that the network-connectivity axes are stiff (stiff fraction $0.83$ / $0.87$) and the single-cell biophysical axes sloppy ($0.08$ / $0.16$), so ``connectivity stiff, single-cell sloppy'' reproduces across independent implementations of the same organism (Supplementary~\S6.6). The same split holds across four models in two phyla, and the crustacean reaches it from the other direction: among the $2365$ enumerated pyloric conductance sets the least variable are the graded inhibitory synapses and the PY A-current and the most variable are the leak, H and CaT currents, so a variability ranking and a curvature ranking both place coupling constrained and the fast inward currents free, while leak sits on opposite sides in the two systems (Supplementary~\S5.8).



\subsection*{Convergent observations in independent biological data}
Four datasets that use none of our forward models show the same structure at four levels of description (Fig.~\ref{fig:external}). 
\emph{(1) Pyloric degeneracy.} Here the behaviourally equivalent set is enumerated rather than inferred: $2365$ conductance sets produce the same triphasic rhythm \citep{prinz2004similar} and span $23.2$ of $31$ dimensions, so fixing the rhythm leaves most of the conductance space free, structured by correlation among $1.1\%$ of pairs. Its response saturates in the step for both observable sets, so it fails the second gate and no curvature dimension is reported for it (Supplementary~\S2.1).
\emph{(2) Neuronal property variability.} Across $n{=}1914$ Allen cortical neurons the rate and shape properties span a coefficient of variation from $0.34$ to $1.85$ on a manifold of participation dimension $4.78$ of $11$, the membrane-potential properties being excluded since that measure depends on the zero of a millivolt scale.
\emph{(3) Gene conservation.} Across $40$ \emph{C.~elegans} ion-channel and synaptic genes, ortholog identity spans $77.9$--$99.9\%$, the most conserved being the gap-junction gene \texttt{unc-9}, consistent with connectivity being stiff in both worm models (ordering-level, not gene by gene; Supplementary~\S2.3).
\emph{(4) Behavioural variation.} Across $n{=}61$ isogenic N2 worms ($683$ Tierpsy features), cross-individual behaviour occupies a participation dimension of $9.75$: genetically identical animals vary on a roughly ten-dimensional manifold. What biology conserves and what it permits to vary are ordered consistently with the models' identifiable and degenerate axes.

% ============ DISCUSSION ============
\section*{Discussion}
We report a measurement of what behaviour can and cannot identify in whole-organism nervous-system models. In the nematode model built on the connectome, behaviour constrains eight of three thousand connection directions, and the conductances it barely sees are the calcium currents EGL-19 and UNC-2. Measuring further aspects of one action adds little: in the fly the behaviours compared are descending turn commands driving one pattern generator, in the nematode touch at two sites and two intensities, and the subspaces they determine overlap far more than they complement. Actions that engage different circuits behave otherwise: chemotaxis and locomotion render different axes stiff, four directions each and six together, the two additional directions entering at the scale of the third and fourth. Escape and feeding remain untested, for want of a simulator that drives them independently, a requirement on the models before it is a question about the animals.

The organising principle is that behaviour reports on a small, structured part of a model. Any one behaviour pins a few stiff parameter combinations, and different behaviours pin the combinations their function requires; between aspects of one action the subspaces overlap heavily and their combined curvature saturates at once.

This recasts degeneracy as a measurable resource rather than a fixed limit, and says where parameter recovery should be posed. What behaviour identifies is a few coordinates on a parameter vector rather than the vector itself; in the cell models where the truth is known, the identified models are recovered exactly and the degenerate one is not. The identified set grows with the number of functionally distinct actions a simulator can be driven through, making assay diversity rather than recording duration the quantity to optimise, but it does not grow without limit: after the whole repertoire has been used a residual degenerate core remains, and those are the candidate neutral directions.

The result lifts the sloppy-models picture \citep{gutenkunst2007universally,transtrum2015perspective,sethna2010sloppy} and pyloric degeneracy \citep{prinz2004similar,marder2011variability} from single circuits to behaving whole organisms, and explains why behaviour-based calibration recovers a working parameter set without unique biophysical values: it recovers the combined stiff subspace, not the core. That two connectome-constrained models in different phyla, one visual and one motor, both concentrate their curvature in under five per cent of sampled directions indicates the number reflects what behaviour transmits, not one simulator.

How much was recorded sets what can be found, and by how much is now measurable. A Gauss--Newton curvature built from $n_{\mathrm{obs}}$ observables has rank at most $n_{\mathrm{obs}}$, and the models track that bound: reducing the visual model's output channels moves its effective dimension almost one for one with the channel count, and the FlyGym dimension rises from $1$ to $11$ when ten summary scalars are replaced by $4200$ joint angles. An effective dimension computed from fewer observables than parameters is therefore a property of the assay, and the same holds for a step size below the proportional range or a signal below the simulator's own noise. Every dimension here carries its observable count, step-size scan and noise floor, and the three conditions have teeth: they retire the pyloric circuit, whose response saturates in the step for every observable set we tried, and they move the fly figure from $16$ to $11$ once it is measured where the response is proportional.

Three limitations bound these results. Each dimension is conditional on a choice of observables, which no principle fixes uniquely. The conductance decomposition resolves mechanism classes rather than individual cells, so it names which currents behaviour leaves free and not which cells express them. And behaviour locates a perturbation only to within a set: the mechanisms it can detect are mutually confusable at cosine above $0.9$, and the alignment with the biological datasets holds at the level of dimensionality and direction rather than eigenvector by eigenvector (Supplementary~\S5.4, \S5.5).

The strongest outstanding test is prospective: predicting from the model which perturbations should produce the largest behavioural change, and testing that ordering on data not used to build it. Such a test requires perturbations resolved at the coupled parameter combinations the geometry identifies rather than at single genes or channels, and defining those endpoints in advance protects the statistics when independent samples are few.

Behavioural identifiability is therefore a quantity that can be measured rather than assumed, and its extent is set by the diversity of behaviours elicited. The stiff subspace is the part of a fitted model that the assay pins down and that supports biological interpretation; the free directions name what it leaves open and which further behaviours would close. The same geometry prices an assay before it is run: one that adds another aspect of the same action re-measures known combinations, one that changes what the circuit must do reaches new ones. This applies to any mechanistic simulator fitted to behaviour.

\section*{Methods}
\textbf{Manifold drift.} For each simulator, $N$ optimisations (separable CMA-ES or SPSA) from independent starts are fitted to one fixed behavioural target; we report behavioural recovery, the normalised per-dimension dispersion of the $N$ recovered parameter vectors, and the PCA participation dimension of the solution cloud. \textbf{Behavioural curvature.} A finite-difference Jacobian can be used for attribution, mapping a behavioural residual onto the mechanism that controls it \citep{he2026pgob}, or for identifiability, which is what is measured here. All curvature reported here is the Gauss--Newton form $H=J^\top W J$, built from the finite-difference observable Jacobian $J_{ik}=\partial\,\mathrm{obs}_i/\partial\theta_k$ and a diagonal positive semi-definite whitening matrix $W$ of inverse per-observable scales. $H$ is therefore positive semi-definite by construction and coincides with the Fisher information under a Gaussian observation model whose precision is $W$, so its eigenvalues are non-negative and its effective dimension is the local practical-identifiability rank in the standard sense \citep{villaverde2014reverse,gutenkunst2007universally}. Eigenvalues are obtained by direct eigendecomposition (low dimension) or Lanczos iteration (high dimension). The effective dimension at level $q$ is the smallest $k$ such that $\sum_{i\le k}\lambda_i \ge q\sum_i \lambda_i$ with eigenvalues sorted in decreasing order; we report $q=0.90$ and $q=0.99$, together with the threshold-free participation ratio $(\sum_i\lambda_i)^2/\sum_i\lambda_i^2$ (Supplementary~\S6.9). Curvature is evaluated at the published operating point, and its point dependence is measured directly: on the connectome-scale model, four displaced operating points give effective dimension $8$--$12$ with stiff subspaces coinciding across points at cosine $0.89$ against a random null of $0.15$ (Supplementary~\S5.3); on modWorm, five displaced optima and twenty-four random points all give a low effective dimension (Supplementary~\S6.8). A direction counts as flat when $\lambda$ falls to the finite-difference floor; residual eigenvalues of magnitude below that floor, of either sign, are numerical noise and are treated as zero. We adopt it as the primary cross-simulator metric because it is robust to the choice of observable and parameterisation, unlike a raw perturbation-sensitivity ratio, and because the multistart manifold-drift analysis provides an independent, optimisation-based cross-check that agrees with it ($\rho{=}0.96$). \textbf{Eigenworm identity.} Real \emph{C.~elegans} skeletons (OWMD N2) and model body postures are reduced to tangent-angle eigenmodes by PCA; we report the posture effective dimension, the top-four cumulative variance, and the principal-angle cosine between model and real eigenworm subspaces, with significance assessed against random subspaces of matched dimension. \textbf{Complementary tiling and saturation.} Per-behaviour stiff subspaces are taken as the leading Hessian eigenvectors under each behavioural target; we report pairwise stiff-subspace cosines, the effective dimension of the summed per-behaviour curvatures, and its saturation against the number of behaviours. Context dependence (BAAIWorm) is the ratio of a parameter class's behavioural sensitivity between two behaviours (chemotaxis versus weak-gradient locomotion). The same summed-curvature construction is applied to the rodent (Virtual Rodent / dm\_control). \textbf{Cell-scale ground truth.} Eleven published cell models (five neuronal, six biochemical oscillators) are measured with the identical estimator and recovered from random starts $\theta^{*}e^{\,\mathcal{U}(-\ln f,\ln f)}$ at $f{=}1.5$--$10$, with a phase-invariant feature loss computed at full resolution; a fit counts as converged below behavioural distance $0.02$ and only converged fits contribute parameter errors (Supplementary~\S7). \textbf{Calibration.} Per-dimension rank-based simulation-based calibration \citep{talts2018validating,hermans2022sbc} of an amortised neural posterior; a dimension passes when the per-axis Kolmogorov--Smirnov test is non-significant at $\alpha{=}0.05$. \textbf{External datasets.} The Prinz--Marder pyloric population, measured as the geometry of the $2365$ published conductance sets that produce the canonical rhythm rather than as a curvature, since its response saturates in the step; Allen Cell Types electrophysiology; WormBase ParaSite / Ensembl Metazoa ortholog identities; and OpenWorm Movement Database isogenic-N2 Tierpsy features. All four are public and independent of the simulators measured here. Full specifications, cohort sizes and reproduction commands are given in the Supplementary Information.
