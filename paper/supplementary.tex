\section{Measurement definitions and assay specifications}
\subsection{Sensitivity, spectral mass and numerical rank}
An assay specifies a forward model, its parameter point, an input and an output vector. The sensitivity Jacobian is the derivative of that output in the stated parameter coordinates. We approximate it by finite differences and standardize output rows using reference scales. The positive semi-definite Gram matrix $H=J^{\top}WJ$ measures squared output sensitivity. It has the Fisher-information interpretation only for a Gaussian observation model with known precision $W$; most assays here use output-normalization weights.

The concentration counts $d_{90}$ and $d_{99}$ describe how many leading eigenvalues accumulate $90\%$ and $99\%$ of the trace. The participation ratio is $(\sum_i\lambda_i)^2/\sum_i\lambda_i^2$. These measures summarize the relative distribution of sensitivity. Numerical rank is evaluated separately from the complete spectrum at an explicit tolerance, and parameter recovery is assessed by fitting. Resolved weak directions can lie outside the selected concentration count. Silent individual Jacobian columns and insensitive linear combinations are also different objects.

For positive parameters, multiplicative steps $\theta_i(1\pm\delta)$ and log steps $\theta_i e^{\pm\delta}$ both approach log-coordinate derivatives as $\delta\to0$. At finite $\delta$, they define distinct approximations. Signed biases require an affine scale, and zero-valued nonnegative parameters lie at a domain boundary. The visual-model analysis treats these cases explicitly (\S4). Under a coordinate change $q=Tz$, the Gram matrix becomes $T^{\top}H_qT$; spectra therefore depend on the coordinate metric \citep{white2016limitations}.

\section{Finite-difference and realization diagnostics}
\subsection{Nematode circuit}
Two baseline simulations reproduce the full-assay voltage exactly. Four connections are selected by their full-assay column norms: strongest, median, lower quartile and weakest nonzero. Across relative steps $0.05$, $0.1$, $0.25$ and $0.5$, the step-$0.5$ derivatives differ from the step-$0.05$ estimates by $0.0061\%$, $0.196\%$ and $8.27\%$ for the first three columns. The weakest column is zero at $0.05$ and gives the same quantized response at the three larger steps. Its derivative is unresolved. These $34$ rollouts support the sampled strong and median responses. The complete $3076$-column spectrum has not been evaluated at smaller steps. Across the tested steps, the sampled strong and median columns agree within $0.2\%$.

A separate connection control compares the standardized response to one weight perturbation at steps $0.05$, $0.1$, $0.25$, $0.5$ and $1.0$. Response divided by step is approximately $2.84\times10^{-4}$ throughout, with relative variation below $0.7\%$. Identical baseline simulations agree bit for bit. Repeatability and approximate proportionality hold for this sampled connection under its specified protocol.

\subsection{Fly walking controller}

\subsection{Oscillator and discontinuous locomotor assays}
In the pyloric implementation, a pointwise trace response is approximately constant across a $250$-fold perturbation range. Its finite-difference Jacobian consequently scales approximately as the inverse step. Phase shifts and finite recording windows can produce this response, so the tested pointwise differences do not resolve a stable local derivative. The tested phase-invariant summaries also fail response proportionality: the response-to-step ratio varies by $23.2$ across a $25$-fold step range. We use the accepted parameter population to describe parameter variation compatible with the selected rhythm (\S6).

The modWorm posture response is discontinuous under the tested implementation and stimulus. A synaptic rise-time perturbation produces negligible change at $0.1\%$ and $1\%$, then an output change of $0.141$ at both $5\%$ and $10\%$. Two of seven mechanism gains have changes near numerical precision at the finite step. Its sensitivity counts describe finite-step responses under these settings.

Repeated larvaworld baselines show substantial realization variability. The stored median perturbation signal-to-noise ratio is $1.21$, and three tested parameters are below their own baseline-variation floor. Its single-realization spectrum is excluded from the primary concentration comparison.

\section{Motor-output sensitivity and stimulation contexts}
\subsection{Connection weights and output layers}
The fixed-input assay drives the fifteen sensory inputs with presynaptic voltage $u_i(t)=2\sin[2\pi(0.5t+i/15)]$ in mV, with $t$ in seconds. NEURON uses $\mathrm{d}t=5/3$ ms, $v_{\mathrm{init}}=-65$ mV and $\mathrm{secondorder}=0$, over $2000$ ms. The $80$ motor outputs are sampled at $120$ points each. Relative connection steps are $\pm0.5$; the matrix records the centered output difference divided by the perturbation interval in dimensionless relative coordinates. Output rows are divided by each baseline neuron's temporal standard deviation, floored at $10^{-3}$ mV. The complete connection-weight assay gives $26/66$, participation ratio $6.83$ and no column below the recorded response threshold. At the floating-point Gram tolerance, $932$ eigenvalues are numerically resolved. The tolerance measures arithmetic resolution; parameter recovery is evaluated by fitting.

An earlier assay with incompletely specified input and rollout gives $8/47$ and participation ratio $2.55$. Applying its float32 arithmetic to the fixed-input responses yields $26/66$, so numerical precision does not explain the discrepancy. The equivalence of these two assays cannot be established. The primary spectrum uses the fully specified fixed-input assay.

The drive sets the synapse's presynaptic voltage. Each external input connection has fixed dimensionless gain $10$; these added input gains are outside the perturbed internal connection vector.

An individual column is classified as numerically inactive when
\[
\|K_i\|_2<10^{-12}\max(\max_j\|K_j\|_2,10^{-30}).
\]
No step-$0.5$ column meets this arithmetic cutoff. The smallest column remains unresolved in the smaller-step control in \S2.

The internal connection vector contains $1341$ negative inhibitory-synapse weights, $651$ positive synapse weights and $1084$ positive gap-junction weights, with no zero weight. Multiplicative steps preserve each sign. Their relative coordinate locally changes log magnitude while preserving connection sign.

Chemical synapses contribute $69\%$ of squared motor-voltage sensitivity from $1992$ weights, compared with $31\%$ from $1084$ gap-junction weights. The mean squared sensitivity per chemical connection is $1.22$ times the gap-junction value, while the median column norm is $6.62$ times greater. The difference between these ratios shows that typical connection sensitivity and total class contribution describe different aspects of the distribution. These measurements use the fixed-input voltage assay. The overview trace displays baseline motor voltage after the initial transient, using the neuron with the largest temporal standard deviation over the displayed window.

The muscle readout maps motor voltages to preactivation $Vw$ and muscle activation $\mathrm{clip}((Vw+80)/100,0,0.8)$. Both are derived from the same motor-voltage perturbation rollouts using the model's retained readout weights. Each has $96\times120=11{,}520$ output components and its own per-muscle temporal standard deviation, floored at $10^{-3}$ in that observable's units. Complete spectra give $3/9$ for preactivation and $5/17$ for clipped activation. The contrasts reflect both the output map and its reference normalization. The separate earlier muscle-readout count $3/11$ has no verified clipped-activation interpretation.

Each displaced parameter vector multiplies every weight independently by a factor drawn uniformly in log space between the reciprocal and the stated fold width; replicate labels denote independent draws. The same $300$-connection subset at the reference and four displaced parameter points has $d_{90}=8$--$12$ and $d_{99}=28$--$30$. Mean pairwise leading-subspace cosine is $0.89$, with range $0.78$--$1.00$; the matched random control is $0.162\pm0.019$. The full-vector displacement changes every weight, but the sensitivity matrix is recomputed only on this common subset. This assay uses a different rollout and output normalization from the full-connectome assay.

\subsection{Named conductance gains}
Each conductance variable is assigned to its containing NEURON membrane mechanism. In particular, the same SLO conductance variable can occur in distinct EGL-19- or UNC-2-coupled mechanisms. Each gain acts on the conductance variable within its specified membrane mechanism. Uniform class gains change all compartments expressing the named mechanism together.

The sixteen-gain assay gives $d_{90}=3$, $d_{99}=6$ and participation ratio $2.43$. Its largest column norms are IRK $123.5$, NCA $117.7$, EGL-36 $86.0$ and EGL-2 $44.6$; EGL-19 is $1.77$ and UNC-2 $0.17$. Every gain responds. The complete eigenvalues range from $2.26\times10^{4}$ to $1.50\times10^{-3}$, above the stated floating-point tolerance of $2.26\times10^{-8}$. Eigenvector participation ranges from $2.88$ for the leading combination to $1.02$ for the weakest. A floating-point tolerance is distinct from a finite-difference convergence bound. These values characterize finite-step responses; the conductance-class spectrum has not been tested across perturbation steps.

Per-cell granularity changes the interpretation of uniform gains. A modWorm finite-step leak probe over $279$ neurons gives $d_{90}=1$ and $d_{99}=2$, but its uniform direction carries only $0.295\%$ of the trace. This is a finite-step finding under the discontinuous implementation described in \S2. The class-gain ranking therefore cannot specify which cells carry the effect.

\subsection{Combined stimulation conditions}
The paired sensory-versus-motor perturbation comparison gives sensory output changes $0.872$ and $0.122$ with food close and far, and motor changes $0.109$ and $0.357$. These quantities belong to this paired experiment; its baseline displacements differ from the separately computed class-gain union experiment.

The closed-loop assays perturb $20$ global gains or $14$ neuron-class leak gains in log coordinates at step $0.25$. Each of two food locations supplies six path statistics over $120$ physics updates. Rows are normalized by $|y_i^{\mathrm{baseline}}|+10^{-9}$ separately for each condition. Stacking these standardized Jacobians gives $K_{\mathrm{union}}$ and $H_{\mathrm{union}}=H_{\mathrm{chemo}}+H_{\mathrm{loco}}$. Both assays use the same closed-loop physics implementation. The path statistics of both reference trajectories reproduce the previously reported baselines exactly.

At relative singular-value thresholds $10^{-4}$ and $10^{-6}$, each experiment has $4$ and $4$ single-condition directions and $8$ combined directions. Floating-point cutoffs also resolve small residual singular values; this arithmetic resolution differs from a measured simulation-noise floor. At the reported tolerance, the complete spectra resolve eight combined sensitivity directions. The $20$-gain counts are $2/4$, $1/2$ and $2/4$; the $14$-class counts are $2/4$, $1/2$ and $1/4$, respectively. Combined eigenvalues beyond rank four carry $0.73\%$ and $0.30\%$ of their total squared sensitivity. These tail fractions use the entire spectrum and do not identify a fixed set of newly added parameter directions.



In the combined-condition eigenvalue panel, values below $10^{-10}$ of each leading eigenvalue are omitted on the logarithmic axis without substituting a plotting floor. All twenty eigenvalues enter the concentration-count denominators. Numerical ranks use singular-value tolerances, separately from the plotting cutoff.

For four FlyGym descending commands, subset-averaged combined $d_{90}$ values are $12.25$, $12.67$, $12.50$ and $13.00$ as the number of commands increases from one to four; corresponding $d_{99}$ values are $30.75$, $31.67$, $32.00$ and $32.00$. \section{Visual-network state and parameter coordinates}
The analysis uses flyvis $1.1.2$ and one official pretrained flow network. Model identity and library versions are specified with the result files. The matching initialized control uses the same connectome, stimulus, parameter-index ordering and fixed bias scale. Each simulation first computes a one-second grey steady state, then a twenty-frame sinusoidal luminance movie at $0.02$ s per frame. Neural activations are averaged over the last five frames and reduced to contiguous neuron-index bins. The luminance input is $0.5+0.5\sin[2\pi(i/40-0.1f)]$, with flattened input index $i$ and frame index $f$; it is not assigned a verified two-dimensional spatial geometry. Each output bin groups a contiguous range of neuron indices.

All $734$ parameters are perturbed. The primary subset omits the $31$ synaptic strengths that are zero in the trained state, leaving $703$ shared interior coordinates. Positive parameters satisfy $\theta=\theta_0e^q$; biases satisfy $\theta=\theta_0+q$ with fixed unit scale. At a zero strength, the separate boundary analysis uses $\theta=sq$, $q\ge0$, with $s$ equal to the corresponding initialized strength and a second-order right derivative. Its Gram matrix measures sensitivity along the feasible, one-sided boundary directions.

For each bin count, the state comparison uses the trained reference scales $s_i=|y_i^{\mathrm{trained}}|+10^{-6}$ for both states. At $64$ bins, the matched initialized and trained counts are $15/34$ and $9/22$. At $32$, $16$, $8$ and $6$ bins, they are respectively $3/11$, $2/8$, $2/6$, $1/5$ for initialization and $2/7$, $2/5$, $1/5$, $1/4$ for the trained network. Normalizing each state by its own baseline instead gives $43/58$ for initialization and $9/22$ for the trained state. The primary state comparison holds the trained-reference weights fixed; state-specific normalization defines a separate comparison.

At $64$ bins, all $64$ singular directions exceed the tolerance $\epsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}\max(n,p)\sigma_1$. The concentration counts $9/22$ summarize how squared sensitivity is distributed within this resolved span. Including the boundary directions leaves the trained $64$-bin counts unchanged. The result files include complete spectra, raw and standardized Jacobians, parameter definitions and readout boundaries for each step and bin count.

The trained concentration counts remain $9/22$ at parameter steps $0.003$, $0.01$ and $0.03$, with aggregate Jacobian differences below $1\%$. With the physical movie and output scales fixed, integration steps $0.02$ s, $0.01$ and $0.005$ s give concentration counts $8/22$ and $8/21$ at the two finer steps. The trained Jacobian differs from its step-$0.01$ estimate by $0.58\%$ at step $0.003$ and $0.81\%$ at $0.03$. At the smaller step, $55$ full-matrix columns differ by more than $10\%$, including numerical-zero and weak columns; they account for $0.0026\%$ of primary squared sensitivity. These columns contribute little to total squared sensitivity, although their individual derivatives remain unresolved. Repeat baseline differences are at most $6.7\times10^{-16}$. The neuron dynamics use $\max(\tau,\mathrm{d}t)$; $51$ trained time constants lie below the $0.02$-s step, so some weak derivatives reflect this implementation floor. Activation transitions can also affect finite-step derivatives. Integration-step convergence is assessed separately below.

A separate protocol gives $14/32$ using $330$ raw coordinates in an initialized network.

Posthoc integration controls hold the same sinusoidal luminance movie for $0.4$ s, the initial grey input for $1$ s, each original frame for $0.02$ s, and the final averaging window for $0.1$ s. Using the same $703$ coordinates and the output scales from the native $0.02$-s baseline, steps $0.02$, $0.01$ and $0.005$ s give $9/22$, $8/22$ and $8/21$. Jacobians at the finer steps differ by $8.90\%$ and $11.11\%$ from the native estimate, and by $2.56\%$ from each other. These controls also release time constants held at the native implementation floor, so they change the effective implemented dynamics as well as integration resolution. The trained model uses $0.02$ s. Renormalizing each finer-step output by its own baseline instead gives $2/3$ and $1/2$, because some bin means shrink substantially; these changed-weight results are not used for the fixed-metric integration comparison.

\section{Known-truth fitting and recovery}
\subsection{Model panel and fitting protocol}
The panel contains five neuronal models and six biochemical oscillators: Hodgkin--Huxley, A-current Hodgkin--Huxley, Wang--Buzs\'aki, Morris--Lecar and FitzHugh--Nagumo; MAPK, repressilator, Goodwin, Selkov, Brusselator and Goldbeter \citep{hodgkin1952quantitative,connor1971prediction,wang1996gamma,morris1981voltage,fitzhugh1961impulses,kholodenko2000negative,elowitz2000synthetic,goodwin1965oscillatory,selkov1968self,prigogine1968symmetry,goldbeter1991minimal}. Equations, fixed constants, initial conditions and output indices are specified in the accompanying implementations. Generating traces are produced by these implementations; the recovery experiment tests their parameter inference under the stated assays.

Generating parameters are the specified model defaults. The random start is $\theta_i^*e^{\eta_i}$, with $\eta_i$ independently uniform in $[-\ln f,\ln f]$ for $f=1.5$, $3$ and $10$. This distribution uses the truth as its center. Settled traces supply amplitude quantiles, Fourier magnitudes and autocorrelation features computed before reduction. Most models discard $40\%$ of the trace; the A-current neuron discards $50\%$ to avoid its silent interval. CMA-ES uses population $16$, at most $400$ iterations, and the specified seed-dependent initialization. Loss below $0.02$ defines fit success.

The reported parameter error is $\|\hat\theta-\theta^*\|_2/\|\theta^*\|_2$, computed in the units of the implemented parameter vector. This metric is not invariant to rescaling individual coordinates with different units. We compare recovery within each model using this error and the per-parameter ratios. Parameter medians in the panel condition on successful output fits, and fit fractions are shown separately. In the cross-model parameter-recovery plot, marker area is $15+45r$ points squared, where $r$ is the fraction of successful fits. Errors are plotted at their measured values without a plotting floor.

\section{Additional analyses and reproduction}
\subsection{Postural modes and finite-step controls}
Real nematode posture has participation ratios $3.79$ and $4.14$ under two skeleton resolutions; four leading modes account for $96\%$ and $94\%$ of variance \citep{stephens2008dimensionality}. Model--data leading-subspace cosines are approximately $0.77$ for modWorm and $0.68$--$0.69$ for BAAIWorm. These comparisons describe posture variation in output space. The posture displacement ratio for stiff versus weak mechanism perturbations is $1.17$, a modest descriptive contrast.

Finite-step repertoire probes in modWorm and a rodent controller have different subset curves under their respective parameter and output definitions.

\subsection{Fit dispersion and posterior calibration}
Multistart solution clouds measure dispersion among parameter fits at a specified optimization budget and output tolerance. A unique-optimum control also produces a dispersed cloud before optimization finishes, so the PCA dimension of these clouds does not establish an exact equivalence manifold.

Simulation-based calibration tests the calibration of an inferred posterior \citep{talts2018validating,hermans2022sbc}. A prior-like posterior on an uninformative parameter can pass this test, so pass counts do not identify sensitive directions. The modWorm check evaluates posterior calibration under the specified simulation and inference procedure.

\section{Prospective stimulus comparison}\label{sec:prospective}
\subsection{Synthetic observations and fitting}
We generated $12$ independent parameter vectors for the point-neuron circuit by drawing each of its $16$ log conductance gains uniformly from $[-0.2,0.2]$ around the published reference. Each voltage trajectory records $80$ motor cells at $129$ times over $1500$ ms, with integration step $5/3$ ms. Observations add independent Gaussian noise with standard deviation $1$ mV. Each strategy receives one baseline trajectory and one additional trajectory. Within a case, strategies share the baseline observation and the same additional noise array; cases use independent noise arrays. The primary endpoint is voltage root-mean-square error (RMSE) against the noiseless generating response to one excluded test stimulus. The secondary endpoint is RMSE in natural-log conductance gains.

Fits minimize half the summed squared voltage residuals divided by the noise variance, plus a Gaussian log-gain penalty with standard deviation $0.35$. Each log gain is bounded by $\pm\ln 2$. The baseline uses the public reference and an independently perturbed reference as its two starts. All additional-data fits share the best baseline fit and the second baseline fit as their two starts; these can coincide. The trust-region solver uses central differences at log step $0.001$, initial radius $0.05$, maximum radius $0.2$, and at most $50$ iterations per start. A proposed step must reduce the actual objective and achieve an actual-to-predicted improvement ratio above $0.1$. It stops after a small improvement at training RMSE at most $1.02$ mV, a step smaller than $0.001$ at RMSE at most $1.05$ mV, no accepted step, or the iteration cap. Training RMSE above $1.05$ mV marks a fit-quality flag, and flagged outcomes are included in all endpoint summaries. Simulation costs can differ despite equal measurement budgets and iteration caps.

\subsection{Candidate stimuli and selection}
The baseline steps external presynaptic input to $-5$ mV in AWAL, AWAR, AWCL and AWCR from $250$ to $1000$ ms. The candidate library contains this stimulus and steps to $-5$ or $+5$ mV in each of five groups: the baseline group; ASKL, ASKR, PHAL and PHAR; ALNL and ALNR; PLML; and URYDL, URYDR, URYVL and URYVR. Two further candidates ramp to $+5$ mV in the baseline or ASKL/ASKR/PHAL/PHAR group over the same interval. Inactive input is $-65$ mV. Non-repeat strategies select from the $11$ candidates other than baseline. The excluded test stimulus ramps URY inputs to $+2$ mV from $200$ to $950$ ms, then steps PLML to $-2$ mV from $1050$ to $1250$ ms. Neither this response nor the generating parameters enters selection.

For each baseline fit, we construct $16$ compatible model vectors, including the best fit. Gaussian proposals use the inverse of the regularized local sensitivity precision, with fixed proposal scales $1$, $0.5$ and $0.25$. Proposals must satisfy the parameter bounds and have nonlinear penalized objective within $13.148$ of the best fit. These proposals form an approximate baseline-compatible ensemble; they are not exact posterior samples. For a candidate, let $C$ be the sample covariance of its predicted voltage vector across these models. The ensemble-selection strategy maximizes $\frac12\log\det(I+C/\sigma^2)$, evaluated through the equivalent model-space determinant, where $\sigma=1$ mV. This is a Gaussian ensemble information proxy. The largest-response control maximizes root-mean-square voltage change from the corresponding unstimulated responses, averaging squared changes across models, motor cells and time. The other controls repeat the baseline or choose a candidate uniformly at random.

\subsection{Results and uncertainty}
All $12$ cases completed baseline fitting and ensemble construction, giving $48$ strategy outcomes and $24236$ retained forward responses. Table~\ref{tab:prospective} includes all outcomes. One ensemble-selected fit ended at the iteration cap with training RMSE $2.622$ mV after $50$ iterations. Its excluded-stimulus RMSE was $0.986$ mV. Figure~\ref{fig:si_prospective} displays all cases. Uncertainty intervals use $10000$ paired bootstrap resamples of the independent parameter cases, preserving the shared noise within each case. The independent parameter cases are the statistical replicates. The $95\%$ intervals describe each comparison separately.
